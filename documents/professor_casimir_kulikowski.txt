BIO / RESEARCH SUMMARY:
Board of Governors Professor — one of the most senior and distinguished faculty titles at Rutgers. Long-tenured AI researcher with decades of work in biomedical informatics and knowledge-based systems. Pioneer in early AI research at Rutgers. Despite distinguished research career, student teaching reviews are almost universally negative.

TEACHING STYLE PATTERNS (aggregated from reviews):
- Lectures consist largely of anecdotes from his 1970s research career with minimal current content
- Slides are 50–90 slides long but contain confusing, sparse information
- Recitation sections are where actual learning happens — attend these instead of lecture
- Grading is lenient: easy A if you propose a halfway interesting project
- Exams include material not covered in class
- Last-minute announcements (dropped night before final reported)
- Students consistently report learning nothing substantive despite passing

NOTES:
Only 10% of students would take him again. The tradeoff is clear: easy grade, zero learning. This is particularly damaging for MS students because CS520 (which he sometimes teaches) is a prerequisite for CS536 (Machine Learning). Students who coast through CS520 with Kulikowski often arrive at CS536 without the AI foundations Elgammal's course expects. If learning matters, take CS520 with Boularias instead.
