SECTION 1 — OFFICIAL COURSE DESCRIPTION (from cs.rutgers.edu)
================================================================

An in-depth study of machine learning, to impart an understanding of the major topics in this
area, the capabilities and limitations of existing methods, and research topics in this field.

TOPICS:
Inductive learning, including decision-tree and neural-network approaches, Bayesian methods,
computational learning theory, instance-based learning, explanation-based learning,
reinforcement learning, nearest neighbor methods, PAC-learning, inductive logic programming,
genetic algorithms, unsupervised learning, linear and nonlinear dimensionality reduction,
and kernel methods.

SECTION 6 — TIPS & SURVIVAL NOTES
================================================================

- Do NOT take this course the same semester as another project-heavy course — it dominates your time
- The math is real: linear algebra, probability, calculus are all active prerequisites, not just nice-to-haves
- Elgammal is flexible on deadlines if you communicate — reach out proactively instead of going silent
- Surprise quizzes have been reported — stay current with readings, don't fall behind
- 3-hour lectures are mentally demanding; review notes same day
- Prerequisite: must have completed CS520 or CS530 first — this builds directly on AI fundamentals
- Pairs well with CS534 (Computer Vision) if you want to go deep on applied ML
