BIO / RESEARCH SUMMARY:
Distinguished Professor with multiple prestigious awards including ACM Paris Kanellakis Theory and Practice Award and NSF grants. Internationally recognized researcher in computational complexity and quantum computing. Despite elite research credentials, teaching reviews are overwhelmingly negative.

TEACHING STYLE PATTERNS (aggregated from reviews):
- Frequently absent from class, including missing his own final exam (multiple semesters reported)
- Missed first month of classes in at least one semester (Dec 2025 reviews)
- Final exam worth 40% of grade — major grade-drop risk at end of semester
- "Creative problems" on exams with no lecture coverage to prepare
- Stingiest curve in the department per student reports
- Office hours exist and include review sessions; practice exams closely match real exams (per one positive review)
- Self-study strongly recommended if enrolled

NOTES:
Only 17% of students would take him again — among the lowest in the department. The single positive review praises him when he does show up and holds office hours. The consistent pattern across negative reviews is unreliability and absence. Students who self-study from CLRS or MIT OCW 6.006/6.046 and use office hours report passing without issue.
